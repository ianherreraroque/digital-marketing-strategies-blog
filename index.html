The foundation

What is a digital marketing strategy?

A digital marketing strategy is a coordinated plan for using online channels, content, technology and data to achieve business goals. Instead of posting or advertising without direction, organizations identify the people they want to reach, define measurable outcomes and choose the channels most likely to influence those people.

These strategies help businesses use limited resources intentionally, reach relevant people and learn which activities create value. Effective strategies connect business objectives (sales, leads, retention or brand awareness) to target audiences (their needs, behavior and preferred platforms). Digital analytics allow teams to monitor results and improve campaigns over time.

The strategy toolkit

Six strategies that work together

Each strategy serves a different part of the customer journey. Combine discovery, engagement, conversion and loyalty around the same business goal.

01 / SEO

Search Engine Optimization

Definition: Improving a website’s relevance, usability and technical accessibility to earn organic search visibility.

Objective: Increase qualified unpaid traffic and visibility.

How: Keyword research, helpful pages, clear site structure, mobile usability and credible links.

Audience: People actively searching for solutions.

Application: A local bakery optimizes a page for “custom birthday cakes in Chihuahua.”

KPIs: Organic sessions, rankings, click-through rate, organic conversions. SEO guidance.

In practice: HubSpot’s marketing blog illustrates educational content built around questions prospective customers search for. No particular ranking is claimed here.

02 / SMM

Social Media Marketing

Definition: Building relationships and distributing content through social platforms.

Objective: Awareness, community engagement and traffic.

How: Platform-specific posts, community management, short videos and paid social campaigns.

Audience: Segments defined by interests, demographics and platform habits.

Application: A café shares behind-the-scenes Reels and responds to customer comments.

KPIs: Reach, engagement rate, profile visits, link clicks, leads.

In practice: Duolingo’s Zaria Parvez explains the brand’s playful, platform-specific TikTok approach in a Sprout Social masterclass. This documents how social content can build brand recognition.

03 / CONTENT

Content Marketing

Definition: Creating useful, relevant material that attracts and supports a defined audience.

Objective: Build trust and guide customers toward decisions.

How: Articles, guides, videos, infographics and tutorials organized around audience questions.

Audience: People researching, comparing or learning about a product.

Application: A fitness brand publishes beginner workout guides.

KPIs: Engaged sessions, time on page, downloads, assisted conversions.

In practice: REI’s Expert Advice library helps outdoor enthusiasts learn skills and choose equipment.

04 / EMAIL

Email Marketing

Definition: Sending permission-based messages to subscribers and customers.

Objective: Nurture leads, encourage repeat purchases and improve retention.

How: Segmentation, welcome sequences, newsletters and automated reminders.

Audience: Subscribers and existing customers who opted in.

Application: An online store sends an abandoned-cart reminder with product details.

KPIs: Click rate, conversion rate, unsubscribe rate, revenue per email. Email guidance.

In practice: Westfield Creative sends its “9 Things Sunday” newsletter with useful links for marketers and creatives, as documented by Mailchimp’s case study.

05 / SEM

Search Engine Marketing / Paid Advertising

Definition: Using paid search advertisements to appear for relevant queries; shopping ads can also promote relevant product listings. SEM is used here in its paid-search sense.

Objective: Generate targeted visits and conversions quickly.

How: Select keywords, write ads, set bids and budgets, and optimize landing pages.

Audience: Searchers with immediate purchase or inquiry intent.

Application: A dental clinic advertises for “teeth cleaning appointment.”

KPIs: Cost per click, conversion rate, cost per acquisition, return on ad spend.

In practice: Dune London and NMPi segmented Google Shopping campaigns and used attribution and automated bidding, described in Google’s historical case study.

06 / CREATORS

Influencer Marketing

Definition: Collaborating with creators whose audiences trust their recommendations.

Objective: Expand relevant reach and build social proof.

How: Select aligned creators, agree on disclosures and deliverables, track links or codes.

Audience: Niche communities following relevant creators.

Application: A skincare startup partners with local beauty creators for product demonstrations.

KPIs: Qualified reach, engagement, tracked sales, cost per acquisition.

In practice: Gymshark’s athlete partnership program connects the brand with people who share its fitness values. Paid relationships should be clearly disclosed.

A strategy in context

Café Horizonte,
Chihuahua

Fictional academic case · Proposed 90-day plan

The business and audience

A neighborhood café serves coffee, quick breakfasts and a place to work with Wi-Fi. It wants more weekday orders from students and young professionals.

Target audience: People aged 18–35 who study or work nearby and value a convenient breakfast or coffee stop.

The objective

Increase weekday online-originated orders by 20% over 90 days compared with the preceding 90-day baseline.

This percentage is a proposed target, not a measured result or a guarantee. Record the actual baseline before launch.

Selected strategies, channels and activities

  • Local SEO · Google Business Profile + website: Update opening hours and menu, publish a useful local menu page and request honest reviews after visits.
  • Social + content · Instagram Reels: Publish three short videos a week showing breakfasts, preparation and the café’s study-friendly setting. Link to the order page.
  • SEM · Google Search: Test geographically targeted breakfast queries, relevant ad copy and a matching mobile menu page.
  • Email · Opt-in loyalty newsletter: Offer voluntary signup, send a welcome message and a weekly weekday offer, and include an unsubscribe option.

Resources and timeline

Illustrative budget: MXN $6,000 per month: $3,000 paid search, $1,500 content production and $1,500 creative/testing. Total for the three-month plan: MXN $18,000.

Days 1–14: Record the baseline, improve the menu and profile, assign tasks and test tracking. Days 15–60: Run the content, ads and email plan. Days 61–90: Refine successful activities and evaluate the target.

Measurement and expected results

Primary KPI: Weekday online-originated orders. Supporting KPIs: menu-to-order conversion rate, ad cost per attributed order, opt-in email clicks and repeat purchase rate.

Use tagged order links and unique coupon codes; count each order once and review weekly. Compare equivalent weekday periods and note holidays or seasonal effects. Better discoverability and more orders are expected directions, not established results.

Why this mix fits

Local search and paid search reach nearby people with immediate intent. Short videos show the experience to the student audience, while permission-based email encourages repeat visits. The limited geographic focus and modest test budget suit a neighborhood café; orders and acquisition costs keep the plan accountable to its business goal.

Measure what matters

Connect KPIs to business outcomes

Choose indicators that reflect the purpose of the campaign.
Business goalRelevant KPIsDecision supported
Increase awarenessQualified reach, branded searches, impressionsAre relevant people discovering the brand?
Generate demandQualified leads, landing-page conversion rateDoes interest become a useful inquiry?
Drive salesOrders, customer acquisition cost, return on ad spendIs acquisition economically sustainable?
Improve loyaltyRepeat purchase rate, retention, email-attributed revenueAre customers returning?

Conversion rate = conversions ÷ relevant visits × 100.

CPA = campaign cost ÷ attributed acquisitions.

ROAS = attributed advertising revenue ÷ ad spend.

Keep the time period and attribution rules consistent. ROAS measures revenue relative to ad spend; it does not account for all costs or prove incremental profit. See Google’s conversion measurement guide.

The takeaway

Choose for the objective.
Improve with evidence.

Choosing the right strategy matters because budget and attention are limited. Business objectives determine the desired outcome; audience needs and behavior determine which strategies and channels can reasonably deliver it. SEO and content support discovery, social builds relationships, paid search captures intent, email encourages loyalty and creators offer relevant exposure.

For sustained discovery, this blog considers helpful content supported by SEO the most effective foundation today: it answers real questions and creates useful resources that can continue supporting a business over time. This is a reasoned judgment, not a universal ranking or a promise of results; it follows the audience-first principles in Google’s SEO guidance. For immediate orders or repeat purchases, paid search or email may fit better.

Measure conversions, acquisition costs and retention against the original goals. Review those results regularly and adapt the plan: a strategy becomes valuable when its contribution can be explained and improved.
